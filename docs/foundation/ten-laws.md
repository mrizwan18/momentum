Momentum Product Bible
Volume I --- Product
Chapter 4: The Ten Laws of Momentum
Version 1.0
---

Purpose
These laws are the constitution of Momentum.
Every feature, algorithm, notification, animation, and engineering
decision must align with them.
If a proposed feature violates one or more laws, it should be redesigned
or rejected.
---

Law 1 --- Practice Before Perfection
The product optimizes for showing up, not flawless execution.
Reward: - Starting - Finishing - Returning
Not innate talent.
---

Law 2 --- Progress Must Always Be Visible
Every session should leave behind evidence.
Examples: - Recording - Growth score - Practice hours - Timeline -
Reflection - XP - Chapter progress
The user should never wonder whether they are improving.
---

Law 3 --- Every Session Ends With Hope
Regardless of score:
Always end with: - encouragement - one actionable improvement -
tomorrow's mission
Never end with failure.
---

Law 4 --- Recovery Is Part of Mastery
Missing practice is normal.
The system must: - shorten sessions - remove guilt - rebuild momentum
Recovery is celebrated.
---

Law 5 --- One Primary Action
Every screen has exactly one dominant action.
Examples: - Continue Practice - Record - Review - Compare
Avoid competing calls-to-action.
---

Law 6 --- Celebrate Courage
Achievements should recognize:
first recording
first comparison
difficult songs
coming back after a break
Courage is as important as consistency.
---

Law 7 --- Reduce Decisions
The app should answer:
"What should I do next?"
Users should rarely have to plan their own practice.
Decision fatigue reduces practice frequency.
---

Law 8 --- Data Encourages
Metrics exist to educate.
Never weaponize: - streaks - rankings - guilt - shame
Charts should create optimism.
---

Law 9 --- Quiet Products Win
As habits strengthen:
Notifications reduce.
The interface becomes calmer.
The product should gradually disappear into the user's routine.
---

Law 10 --- Users Own Their Journey
Data belongs to the user.
Requirements: - Offline-first - Local-first - Export anytime - Explain
every score - Transparent algorithms
Trust is a feature.
---

Decision Checklist
Before implementing anything, ask:
Does it increase deliberate practice?
Does it reduce friction?
Does it celebrate effort?
Does it support recovery?
Does it preserve trust?
Does it keep the interface calm?
Does it make progress more visible?
If the answer is "no" to multiple questions, reconsider the feature.
---

Chapter Summary
Momentum is not merely software.
It is a philosophy for helping people master difficult skills through
small, repeatable actions over many years.
The Ten Laws exist to ensure the product never loses that purpose as it
grows.
